# Innovator Journal
### Designing for Better Study Habits

**Innovation Design Process | 2026**

*Finding a practical way to reduce interruptions and improve communication between students.*

</div>

---

## 01. Interview

For this project, I interviewed a friend (Jamie) about problems he experiences in his daily life, especially as a student living in a dorm.

During our conversation, we discussed how difficult it can be to stay focused while studying. One major distraction is the phone, but interruptions from friends and roommates can also make it difficult to concentrate.

I learned that students need ways to communicate when they are studying and do not want to be interrupted. At the same time, they should still be able to interact with their friends when they are available.

This helped me realize that the problem was not only about personal concentration, but also about communication with other people.

---

## 02. Principles

Based on the interview, I identified several things that were important to Jamie:

| Principle | Why It Matters |
|:---|:---|
| Simplicity | The solution should be easy to understand and use. |
| Convenience | It should fit naturally into a student's daily routine. |
| Communication | Students should be able to show others when they are studying. |
| Practicality | The solution should not create more distractions or responsibilities. |

### Problem Space

I decided to focus on the problem of students getting interrupted while studying, especially in a boarding school environment where students share dorm spaces and interact with each other frequently.

My goal was to create something that could help students communicate their availability and maintain better focus.

---

## 04. More-Complete Ideas

After reviewing my raw ideas, I selected three concepts to develop into gallery sketches.

### Idea 1 — Smart Phone Lockbox

<div align="center">

![Smart Phone Lockbox Sketch](images/phone-lockbox-sketch.jpg)

*Figure 1. Smart Phone Lockbox concept sketch.*

</div>

**Concept:** A box where students place their phones during study sessions. They can set a timer, and a display shows the remaining time.

**Main Features:**
- Phone storage compartment
- Digital countdown timer
- Start button
- Emergency release

**Why I Considered It:**

It directly addresses phone distractions and encourages students to spend more time focusing on their work.

### Idea 2 — Focus Traffic Light

<div align="center">

![Focus Traffic Light Sketch](images/focus-traffic-light-sketch.jpg)

*Figure 2. Focus Traffic Light concept sketch.*

</div>

**Concept:** A small desk light that communicates a student's current availability through different colors.

**Main Features:**
- Green: Available
- Yellow: Taking a break
- Red: Studying, please do not disturb
- A button to switch between modes

**Why I Considered It:**

It provides a simple visual way for students to communicate with their friends and roommates without needing to explain their availability every time.

### Idea 3 — Smart Study Buddy

<div align="center">

![Smart Study Buddy Sketch](images/smart-study-buddy-sketch.jpg)

*Figure 3. Smart Study Buddy concept sketch.*

</div>

**Concept:** A desk device that tracks study time and encourages students to stay focused through visual progress indicators.

**Main Features:**
- LED progress bar
- Start and pause buttons
- Task completion button
- Presence sensor
- Timer display

**Why I Considered It:**

It encourages students to develop better study habits by giving them visual feedback on their progress.

## 05. Experiment & Path Forward

After comparing the three ideas and discussing them with Jamie, I decided to experiment with the **Focus Traffic Light**.

However, because I had limited time and did not have all the electronic materials needed to build a digital version, I decided to create a simpler paper prototype using colored paper and markers.

### My Prototype

<div align="center">

![Paper Focus Traffic Light Prototype](images/focus-traffic-light-prototype.jpg)

*Figure 4. My paper prototype of the Focus Traffic Light.*

</div>

The prototype uses three different colors to communicate availability:

| Color | Meaning |
|:---:|:---|
| 🟢 Green | Available to talk or hang out. |
| 🟡 Yellow | Taking a break or available briefly. |
| 🔴 Red | Studying and should not be interrupted. |

### Feedback from Jamie

I showed Jamie my paper prototype and asked for his thoughts.

He thought the idea was helpful because it provided a simple way to communicate whether someone was studying or available. However, he also suggested that it would be more useful if there were two signs instead of just one.

He wanted one sign inside his dorm room and another outside the door. This would allow him to see his own study status while also informing his friends before they entered or interrupted him.

I thought this was a useful suggestion because my original design mainly focused on communicating with other people. His feedback helped me realize that the product should consider both the person using it and the people around them.

### Path Forward

Based on Jamie's feedback, I plan to improve the design by creating two matching signs:

1. **Inside sign:** Reminds the student of their current study status.
2. **Outside sign:** Communicates their availability to friends and visitors.

If I have enough time and materials, I would also like to turn the paper prototype into a digital version using LEDs and an Arduino.

My next experiment will focus on testing whether having signs in both locations makes communication easier and actually reduces interruptions.

---

## 06. Reflections

Through this project, I learned that innovation does not always start with inventing something completely new. It can also involve identifying a small problem in everyday life and finding a practical way to improve it.

One of the most useful parts of the process was interviewing someone before deciding on a solution. Initially, I focused mostly on phone distractions and personal concentration. However, after talking with Jamie, I realized that interruptions and communication between roommates were also important parts of the problem.

I also learned that feedback can change an idea significantly. My original Focus Traffic Light only had one sign, but Jamie's suggestion of having one inside and one outside the dorm made me reconsider the design.

Another thing I learned was that a prototype does not need to be complicated or technologically advanced. Even though I could not build the electronic version due to limited time and materials, using paper and colored markers allowed me to test the basic concept and receive useful feedback.

Overall, this experience taught me to focus more on the people who will actually use an innovation instead of just focusing on how interesting or advanced the product might be. In the future, I want to continue testing my ideas, listening to feedback, and making improvements based on real needs.
